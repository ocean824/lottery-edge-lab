Empirically Grounded
- Calculating the exact total number of possible combinations for a specific lottery (e.g., picking 6 numbers from 1 to 40 results in 3,838,380 combinations).
- The brute-force strategy of purchasing every single possible combination to guarantee winning the jackpot.
- The financial formula: Only target lotteries where the jackpot amount is at least three times larger than the total number of possible combinations. This margin is necessary to cover the costs of the tickets, logistical expenses (printing, shipping, couriers), and payouts to investors, while still leaving a profit.

Heuristic but Codeable
- The algorithm required to systematically generate and output every possible number combination for a given lottery format without duplicates.
- The automation of formatting and printing these millions of combinations onto physical lottery tickets using a network of computers and printers.

Marketing Claims
- The proprietary, undisclosed mathematical method referred to as "Combinatorial Condensation."
- The claim that this method could accurately predict 5 out of the 6 total winning numbers.
- The assertion that this technique drastically reduced the probability of winning the lottery from 1 in several million to just 1 in several thousand.

Implementation Ideas
- Establishing a "Lotto Syndicate" to pool financial resources from thousands of individual investors to fund the bulk purchase of tickets.
- Utilizing an era-appropriate tech setup (multiple computers and dozens of printers) to automate the generation and printing of millions of tickets.
- Navigating legal and logistical hurdles by printing tickets in one location (e.g., Sydney, Australia) and shipping them in bulk to the target location (e.g., Virginia, USA).
- Deploying a coordinated network of hired couriers across a state to simultaneously purchase tickets at various retail locations using pre-funded cashier's checks, especially when direct bulk purchasing is restricted.
